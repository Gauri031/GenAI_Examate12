# Nutrition for Young Children (SAMPLE)

## Protein, iron and vitamin A

Growing children need protein in every meal. Good sources are dal, pulses, eggs, milk, curd, paneer, soybean and groundnuts, and meat or fish if the family eats them. Mix cereals with pulses, as in khichdi or dal-roti, for better protein. The exact amount of protein a child needs depends on age and weight, so ask the Anganwadi worker or a doctor for a child's specific requirement.

For iron, give toddlers dal, green leafy vegetables, ragi, dates, eggs, and meat or fish if eaten. A squeeze of lemon on the meal helps absorb iron. For vitamin A give carrot, papaya, mango, pumpkin, spinach, drumstick leaves, eggs and milk.

## Signs of malnutrition

Warning signs in a young child include weight that is low for the age, very thin arms and legs, swelling of both feet, constant tiredness, hair changes and frequent infections or diarrhoea. If you see these signs, take the child to the Anganwadi centre, health centre or a doctor soon. Do not wait for the child to get worse.

## Child cannot take milk

If a child has loose stools, cramps or gas after drinking milk, he may be lactose intolerant. Curd and buttermilk are often easier to digest. Other calcium sources are ragi, sesame seeds and green leafy vegetables. Talk to a doctor before removing milk completely.
